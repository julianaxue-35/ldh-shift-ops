What do the levels mean?

Request check (24–48 h) — green. Can wait. Old-age changes, stable arthritis, overgrown nails, mild skin disease, mild cat flu and still eating.

Urgent (12–24 h) — yellow / orange. Needs a vet look that day or the next. One vomit or loose stool but bright, limping but weight-bearing, not eating (cat 24 h+, dog 48 h+), a wound that may need stitches, repeated vomiting, anything you think is painful.

Emergency (within 2–4 h) — red. Collapse, trouble breathing, pale / yellow / blue gums, major trauma, seizures, suspected toxin, bloat signs, straining to urinate with no urine, heat stress, dystocia, neonate cold or weak. Ticking an emergency sign below sets this level automatically.

FAQ — shelter staff

Quick answers for logging requests and following a case.

Logging requests

Which tab do I use: Vets, Nurses or Behaviour?

All requests are made in the one Send a request card. Pick who it is for: Vets to look at an animal (shelter check, foster, rescue, medication), Nurses for vaccination on arrival (microchip, UV and treatment checks for foster or rescue animals are ticked on the Vets tab), Behaviour for a courthold reassessment, surrender history, aggression / incident or behaviour review. Every form has the same order: What, animal, space and pen, Needed within, then your reason or note. Every tab has an Other choice: describe it in the note. Offsite, Pathway and Vet clinic ask for a detail (for example the foster’s name or the ward) instead of a pen.

How do I log a request for a vet?
  1. In Send a request, pick Vets at the top, then type the Animal ID, choose the space and pen number.
  2. Pick What: Shelter, Foster, Rescue or Medication. For offsite / Pathway animals, add a detail such as the foster's name.
  3. Pick a Suspected condition if you can, and describe the problem in a few words.
  4. Tick any emergency signs. The tier line under them shows how fast the vets will respond.
  5. Pick Needed within and add a short reason or note.
  6. Press Send to vets.
What do the tiers mean?

Emergency (any emergency sign ticked): vets aim to respond within 2–4 hours. Urgent: within 12–24 hours. Request checks (routine): within 24–48 hours. A vet can bump a routine case up to Urgent.

For a true emergency, radio the vet — don't wait for the board. An urgent cat birthing check goes straight to the top of the vet board.

I flagged the same animal twice — what happens?

If the earlier case is still open you'll be told it is already on the board. If it was finished, it is reopened as a fresh flag and the earlier flag is remembered in its history.

How do I ask the nurses for a vaccination, FIV test, blood test, microchip or UV check?

In Send a request, pick Nurses at the top. Choose What, space, species, how many animals and Needed within. Tick Not here yet to schedule it for a later arrival. For a single foster / rescue animal you can also tick Microchip and / or UV on the Vets form — these go to the Processing nurse.

Behaviour tasks and initials

How do I ask the Behaviour team to look at an animal?

In Send a request, pick Behaviour at the top. Choose What (courthold reassessment, owner surrender history with concerns, aggression / incident review, behaviour review, or other), type the Animal ID, choose the space and pen, and choose Needed within. For a behaviour review and an aggression / incident review you must write why you are flagging the animal; for other, describe the issue in the reason box. Enter your initials and press Send to behaviour.

Which “needed within” do I pick?

30–60 minutes is for something that cannot wait (an aggression or incident). Within today is the usual choice. Within 48 hours is for a concern that is not urgent. For a true emergency, radio the team — do not wait for the board.

Who sees my request, and what is “Send straight to the Behaviour TL”?

Every request lands on the Behaviour page in Incoming, and the team then gives it to the TL or the right assist (cat or dog assist, matched to the animal’s space). Tick Send straight to the Behaviour TL only when you want it to skip Incoming and go to the TL at once.

How do I find out what the Behaviour team decided?

When the team finishes, their note shows on that animal’s card on the Shelter board (with their initials and the time) for 24 hours. While the task is open, the Shelter board shows that it is waiting.

Why am I asked for initials, and do I have to type them every time?

Initials show who sent a request, so the team knows who to ask about it. There is one initials box at the top of the Send a request card. It is required for Behaviour and optional for Vets and Nurses (so an emergency is never held up) (they are only added to a flag that goes to the vet board). Your initials are remembered on this device for the day, so you only type them once.

Following a case

Where do I see how a case is going?

In the Cases table. Each row shows the Stage: flagged (waiting for a vet), picked up (a vet has it), waiting for medication (vet check done, medication still to be made up) or completed. Finished cases stay in the Completed list for 24 hours. The Time left column counts down to the tier's target and turns amber, then red, when it is running late.

A case went back from "picked up" to "flagged" — why?

Vets can put a case back if they are called away. It returns to its tier and any vet can pick it up. The time target keeps running from when it was first flagged.

What are the numbers and rings at the top?

The big ring is tasks completed today across all shifts, with a ring per shift. Only today's checks are counted. Older unfinished list entries are shown separately as "older still open" so they don't inflate today's total.

Disease tracking

What is disease tracking, and what are we tracking?

Every time you flag an animal and pick a Suspected condition, that sign is counted. We track eight reported signs: cat flu (URI), kennel cough, diarrhoea / GI upset, vomiting, eye condition, skin condition, wounds / injury and other. The Disease watch card on this page and the full board on the Stats page show, for each space, how many animals were flagged in the last 30 days as a share of that space's cages.

These are signs staff noticed, not diagnoses. A vet diagnosis (for example Giardia) is deliberately not counted here.

Why do we track it?
  • Catch clusters early. Several animals with the same sign in one space is the first clue to an outbreak, often before anyone has diagnosed anything.
  • Back decisions with numbers. Moving animals, tightening cleaning or biosecurity, or isolating a room is easier to justify with a count.
  • Learn our own seasonal pattern. A baseline is being collected for a full year because disease varies with the season. Once it is complete, each period is compared with the same time last year. Until then there is no "high" line.
What do I need to do so the tracking is accurate?
  • Choose a Suspected condition every time you flag an animal. If you leave it blank the animal is flagged but is not counted in disease tracking.
  • Pick the right space and pen — counts are per space.
  • An animal counts once for the same sign, so there is no harm in re-flagging an animal that is still sick.
  • Only flagged animals are counted, so the rate reads lower than a full vet-exam prevalence. Compare it with our own baseline, not with older prevalence figures.
