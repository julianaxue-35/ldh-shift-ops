FAQ — vets

Quick answers for picking up cases, handing work to the nurses and handling results.

Cases

How do I pick up a case?

In Cases, press Pick up → on a card. To take several, tick their boxes and press Pick up N selected, sorted by location. Type your initials once in the box at the top left at the start of the day; picking up uses them (no prompt) and they show on the card (“Picked up 10:32 by AB”), and opens the shift tool for that animal. Once a case is picked up it leaves the pick-up list for the other vets. If another vet got there first you'll be told it was skipped.

I picked a case up but got called away — how do I put it back?

Press Put back on the card. Only the vet who picked it up can put it back. The case returns to its tier column for any vet to pick up. No reason is needed. Its time target keeps running from when it was first flagged, and the card shows "Put back" with the time.

How do I finish a case?

Press DONE once you have seen the animal. For a medication case the DONE panel asks for the medication details, and the case then waits for the nurses to make the label.

How do I hand a case to the nurses?

Press Transfer to nurse on a picked-up case, choose the task (blood test, ear swab, FIV test, IVFT, microchip, UV, other) and add a note. The case leaves the vet board and appears on the Nurses page. Microchip and UV go to the Processing nurse, everything else to the Welfare nurse.

How do I ask the nurses for a task without a case?

Use Send a request and pick Nurses. For What choose Dispense medication (animal, space, pen and the medication in the note; no schedule needed), a one-animal task (blood test, ear swab, FIV test, cat needing IVFT, microchip, UV, treatment check), (for several tasks on the same animal, such as bloods and an FIV test, pick one and tick the others under Also needed for this animal; each goes to the nurses as its own task with the same note), or Treatment course for something given on a schedule such as "flush twice a day for 7 days" (then fill in times a day and days).

How will I know a new request has arrived?

A red NEW REQUEST banner appears at the top, the page plays three beeps and the tab title flashes. It covers requests from shelter staff, results sent back by nurses, and requests from the Behaviour team (the banner says how many came from Behaviour). Tap the page, or the alert sound button, once at the start of your shift so the browser allows sound.

A nurse sent a result back to me — where is it?

It arrives as a normal shelter request in the Cases board, in the Emergency column if the nurse chose "Within 2–4 hours" or the Urgent column for "Within 12–24 hours". The problem line gives the test, the result the nurse wrote, and what is wanted from you: interpret the results, upload them to ShelterMate, or something else (see the comment). Pick it up and press DONE like any other case.

While this page is open, every new request — including a result sent back — raises a red NEW REQUEST banner with one count (for example “3 new requests sent through”), flashes the tab title and beeps. The sound is on by default; browsers need one tap on the page before they will play it, and the sound button at the top mutes it.

Behaviour

The Behaviour team asked me for something — where is it?

Requests from the Behaviour team (shelter stress medication, a post adoption med pack, or another request) arrive on the vet board like any other request, with a note that says it came from Behaviour and a courthold mention if it applies. Shelter stress medication and post adoption packs wait for the nurse’s label like other medication requests.

The Behaviour team finished something I asked for — where is the plan?

In the Behaviour team plans card under Send a request. When Behaviour ticks a task Done, the page rings (the red BEHAVIOUR PLAN banner and sound) and the plan they wrote shows there with who decided and when. It covers tasks given to Behaviour from this page and from the Send to Behaviour button in your shift tools, and stays for 24 hours, so copy anything you need into the animal's notes. While a task is still open the same card says where it is up to. The Shelter board also shows the plan against the animal for 24 hours.

How do I ask the Behaviour team to look at an animal?

In Send a request pick Behaviour. Fill it in top to bottom: What, animal ID, space, pen or detail, Needed within, and a reason (required for a behaviour review and an incident review), enter your initials and send. It lands on the Behaviour page in Incoming. Tick Send straight to the Behaviour TL if you want it to skip Incoming.

I am worried about an animal’s behaviour while writing a note in a shift tool.

In Processing, Sick & Injured or Surgery, press Send to Behaviour on the animal’s card. The animal goes to the Behaviour page with your whole current note attached. The note is deleted from the database when the Behaviour team ticks Done (or within 7 days). Their Done comment shows on the Shelter board.

Do I need to give my initials?

Yes. Whenever you send something to the nurses or to Behaviour, you are asked for your initials. They are remembered on this device for the day.

Disease tracking

What is being tracked and why does it matter to me?

Shelter staff pick a Suspected condition when they flag an animal. The eight reported signs are counted per space against its cages over the last 3 days (Disease watch on the staff page, full board on Stats). The aim is to spot a cluster in one space early, before a diagnosis, so you can act on isolation, cleaning and vaccination decisions sooner.

Should I correct the suspected condition?

The count reflects what staff noticed, not your diagnosis, so there is no need to edit it. Diagnoses such as Giardia are deliberately kept out of the sign list. Please make sure animals are flagged with some condition — blank ones are not counted.

What is the baseline?

Snapshots are taken nightly for a full year because disease changes with the season. After that, each period is compared with the same time last year. Until then rates are shown as they are, with no "high" line.
